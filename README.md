Overview of the project 
 1. A Power BI sales analytics dashboard for a retail or e-commerce business, covering 2019–2020 performance across US regions.
 2. This a Superstore-style retail dataset used to demonstrate business intelligence skills — well-structured with filters, trend analysis, geographic drill-down, and          category breakdown all in one view.


Region Filter (Top Navigation)
Four buttons — Central, East, South, West — act as cross-filters. Selecting any region dynamically updates every visual on the page simultaneously.

KPI Cards (Top Row)
Four headline metrics give an instant snapshot:

Sales: 450K — total revenue for the selected period
Quantity: 6,251 — number of orders placed
Quantity: 53K — total units sold
Quantity: 4 — average items per order

The repeated "Quantity" label suggests these may be differently filtered or calculated variants (e.g., by region or segment), likely connected to the region slicer.


Sub-Category Deep Dive
The Sales by Sub-Category bar chart (bottom right) reveals the top-performing product lines: Phones (60.2K), Chairs (55.8K), and Binders (52.9K). Technology items and furniture pieces rank high despite the category-level data showing lower totals, suggesting fewer but higher-value orders.


>> Left Column — Customer Behavior
Two donut charts reveal how customers engage:

Sales by Segment: Consumer dominates at 50%, followed by Corporate (31%) and Home Office (18%). This tells the business that B2C drives more volume than B2B.
Sales by Payment Mode: COD (Cash on Delivery) leads at 44%, then Online at 31% and Cards at 25%. High COD indicates a market (possibly India or Southeast Asia) where digital payments are still maturing.

>> Center Column — Trend Analysis (YoY)
Two overlapping area charts compare 2019 vs 2020 month by month:

Monthly Sales YoY: 2020 (darker blue) outperforms 2019 across most months. Both years show a strong Q4 (Nov–Dec) spike, typical of holiday/year-end purchasing. There's a visible mid-year plateau around June–August.
Monthly Profit YoY: Profit trends follow sales but are much more compressed in scale (~10K peak vs ~50K+ for sales), revealing tighter margins. A notable dip occurs around March (possibly COVID-19 disruption in 2020), followed by recovery through Q4.

>> Right Column — Operational Mix
Two bar charts show distribution across logistics and products:

Sales by Ship Mode: Standard Class dominates at 109.7K, followed by First Class, Second Class, and Same Day. The heavy reliance on Standard Class suggests customers prioritize cost over speed.
Sales by Category: Office Supplies leads at 0.2M, with Technology and Furniture tied at 0.1M each. This is somewhat surprising — Office Supplies typically has lower unit prices, suggesting very high order volumes.
